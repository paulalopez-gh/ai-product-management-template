## 2. Hallucination gap, what changed

The first model provides an output but doesn’t clearly distinguish between AI-generated decisions and the decisions the PM still needs to make. Once we add the strategic document, the model can make business decisions and prioritize actions based on that strategy, which can create uncertainty. With the new model, we reduce this uncertainty by providing more context and making the AI’s assumptions and decision points clearer, so the PM can better understand what to validate, edit, or decide themselves.

---

## 3. Control gap, what changed

The main change is that the results are now much more aligned with the company’s strategic direction, rather than being based mainly on AI assumptions. This gives the PM more control because the actions are already prioritized according to the company’s guidelines, so I can quickly identify the highest-priority actions without having to review all the strategic documentation first.

---

## Forever takeaway

> The biggest takeaway for me is that the problem isn’t that AI occasionally gets something wrong; it’s that it can hallucinate in a very convincing and reliable-looking way. That makes it especially difficult for users to know when to trust the output, and once trust is lost—particularly with predictive models—it can be very hard to regain.
